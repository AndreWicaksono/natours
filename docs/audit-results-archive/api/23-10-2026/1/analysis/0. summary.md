# Backend Audit Summary: Open Items (Oct 23, 2026)

**Scope**: `apps/api` (NestJS + Prisma back-end)
**Source**: a second-pass review of the fixes from the [08-10-2026 audit](../../../08-10-2026/1/final-result/fixes.md), plus problems found while implementing the resulting fixes
**Related**: [`1. detail.md`](1.%20detail.md) (item-by-item analysis) and [`../final-result/fixes.md`](../final-result/fixes.md) (final state and verification checklist)

## How these items were found

The 08-10-2026 audit's fixes were read file by file before being committed. That review did two things. It found six of the audit's own recommendations to be wrong or incomplete (those were reworked and are documented in the 08-10-2026 report). It also produced a list of **eight problems the audit had not covered, or had marked as correct**. This audit is those eight items, numbered OI-1 to OI-8 in the same order as the "Known open items" list of the 08-10-2026 report, plus eight further problems that surfaced while fixing them.

## Results at a glance

**1 critical, 4 high, 2 medium, 1 low**, plus 8 additional findings.

### 🔴 Critical / High

| # | Severity | Where | Problem |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **OI-2** | Critical | `bookings.service.ts`, `expiration.service.ts`, `stripe-webhook.controller.ts` | **A customer can pay for a booking that already expired.** Seats were held for 15 minutes, but the Stripe Checkout Session stayed payable for 24 hours. A late payment was recorded as `SUCCEEDED` with a `PlatformTransfer`, while the booking stayed `EXPIRED` and its seats were already given away. The customer was charged for nothing. |
| **OI-1** | High | `bookings.service.ts` (`createBookingWithCheckout`) | **Seat overbooking.** The availability check ran outside the transaction and the decrement had no guard, so two concurrent bookings for the last seat both succeeded and `seats_available` went negative. The 08-10-2026 audit had marked this as "Confirmed Correct". |
| **OI-3** | High | `bookings.service.ts` (`cancelBooking`, `resumePayment`) | **A cancelled booking could still be paid**, because its Checkout Session was never closed. `resumePayment` also left the old session open next to the new one, so a customer could pay twice. |
| **OI-4** | High | `partners.controller.ts` (`startOnboarding`, `getConnectStatus`) | **Broken access control.** The routes checked the role but not the partner. Any `PARTNER_ADMIN` could start Stripe onboarding for, or read the Connect status of, any other partner by changing the id in the URL. |
| **OI-5** | High | `tours.service.ts` (`findOne`) | **Personal and account data exposed to every customer** on the tour detail endpoint: the full partner record (Stripe account id, contact email), the email of every reviewer, the tour creator's email, and flagged reviews. |

### 🟡 Medium

| # | Severity | Where | Problem |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **OI-6** | Medium | `schema.prisma` (`Review`) | A leftover `@@unique([tourId, customerId])` still limited a customer to one review per tour **ever**. A customer who booked the same tour twice could not review the second booking, and saw the misleading "already reviewed this booking". |
| **OI-7** | Medium | `bookings.service.ts`, `schema.prisma` (`TransferStatus`) | After a refund, the `PlatformTransfer` row still said `SUCCEEDED` although the partner no longer had the money. `TransferStatus` had no value for a reversed transfer. |

### 🟢 Low

| # | Severity | Where | Problem |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **OI-8** | Low | `stripe-webhook.controller.ts`, `schema.prisma` (`StripeWebhookEvent`) | A narrow idempotency race. A duplicate delivery of an event that was still being processed answered 200; if the first attempt then failed, Stripe considered the event delivered and it was lost. It needs two concurrent deliveries of the same event, which is why it is rated low. |

## Additional findings made while implementing the fixes

| # | Where | Problem |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **A1** | `createBookingWithCheckout` | Price, fee and partner Stripe-account checks ran **after** the booking was created, so a tour with an un-onboarded partner left its seats held until the cron expired the booking. |
| **A2** | `createBookingWithCheckout` | If creating the Stripe Checkout Session failed, a `PENDING` booking kept its seats with no way to pay for it. |
| **A3** | `createBookingWithCheckout` | Two customers booking a brand-new departure date at once collided on the unique `(tour_id, start_date)` index and one got a 500. |
| **A4** | `resumePayment` | The previous session stayed payable (double payment), the new session could outlive the booking, and repeated resumes could hold seats indefinitely. |
| **A5** | `confirmBooking` | Read-then-write, and it silently skipped non-`PENDING` bookings, so the webhook could not tell "already confirmed" from "expired while paying". |
| **A6** | `expireBooking` | Left the payment `PENDING`, and an expiry event about a *replaced* session could expire a live booking. |
| **A7** | Expiration cron | Updated bookings it had read earlier without re-checking their status, so a booking confirmed in between could be overwritten as `EXPIRED`. |
| **A8** | `partners.controller.ts` (`assertCanManagePartner`) | The ownership check added for OI-4 compared `user.partnerId` (a `bigint` at runtime, although `UserPayload` declares `number \| null`) with a `number` using `!==`. That comparison is always true across types, so every partner admin was rejected, including the owner. Found during verification by the "own partner should succeed" control case, and fixed by converting both sides with `BigInt()`. |

## Why the 08-10-2026 audit did not catch these

- **It reviewed modules one at a time.** OI-2, OI-3 and OI-7 only appear when booking state, payment records, webhook handling and Stripe's own timing (a session lives 24 hours by default) are considered together.
- **Its "Confirmed Correct" list was wrong about seats.** Two rows said seat decrement was atomic with no race. The availability check was outside the transaction and the decrement was unguarded (OI-1).
- **Its checklist covered roles but not resources, and not response contents.** Whether a `PARTNER_ADMIN` could act on *another* partner (OI-4) and what a response actually contains (OI-5) were outside what it looked at.
- **It treated the schema as settled.** OI-6 was a constraint left over from before the review feature was redesigned around bookings.

None of this means the earlier audit was wasted: its fixes held up, and several of its items were real bugs. These items sit in the gaps between modules, which a file-by-file read tends to miss.

## Decisions taken

| Decision | Choice | Reason (details in `1. detail.md`) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Seat hold and payment window | 31 minutes, with the Stripe session expiring at the same instant | Stripe's minimum session lifetime is 30 minutes, so the old 15-minute hold could not be matched to the session. One shared expiry removes the gap where a payment can arrive after the hold ends. |
| Payment that arrives after the booking expired | Refund automatically in full | Re-confirming when seats happen to be free gives a nicer experience but needs much more logic. A refund is simple and always safe. |
| Tour detail data | Role-shaped response; reviewer first name and avatar instead of email | Customers need to see who wrote a review, not how to contact them. Admins and partner admins keep the management data they use. |
| Resume of a pending booking | Allowed only while the booking is under 2 hours old | Extending the hold on every resume would otherwise let someone keep seats indefinitely. The 2-hour figure is a judgment call. |
| Duplicate webhook for an event still in progress | Answer 409 | A 200 tells Stripe the event is handled, which loses it if the first attempt fails. |
